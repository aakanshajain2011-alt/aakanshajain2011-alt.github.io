Job Market Paper

  1. The Rise of H-2A: Causal Evidence on Guestworker Substitution for Settled Farm Labor
    Aakansha Jain
    2026
    Abs PDF

    The H-2A agricultural guestworker visa program, which enables U.S. farmers to legally hire foreign labor for seasonal farm jobs, grew more than fourfold between 2008 and 2024. Simultaneously, the settled non-citizen workforce that had long supplied the same seasonal farm labor shrank under intensified immigration enforcement. Two thirds of these settled non-citizen agricultural workers in the U.S. lack work authorization. This paper asks whether the decline in settled farm workers caused the rise of the H-2A visa program, and at what rate. Using a panel of 740 commuting zones, I instrument the contemporaneous settled non-citizen share of agricultural labor with its 2000 baseline share interacted with national enforcement shifts. I find that farms hire about one H-2A guestworker for each settled non-citizen worker lost in headcount, replacing roughly three quarters of the lost labor time. Substitution for lost settled workers accounts for about 29 percent of aggregate H-2A growth over the period.

Working Papers

  1. College Wage Premiums in India: Trends and Spatial Decomposition, 1987-2012
    Aakansha Jain
    2025
    Abs PDF

    This paper traces the college-versus-high-school earnings gap in India across five waves of the National Sample Survey, 1987-2012, a period when tertiary enrollment nearly tripled. I estimate Mincer wage equations and two-part Cragg models that separate labor-force participation from pay, organized into synthetic birth cohorts. The college premium rose substantially throughout the period for both younger and older workers. Zone-level decompositions, motivated by imperfect substitution between young and old workers, show that the young-old premium gap closed where college supply expanded most and persisted where it did not.

  2. An Overview of the 2024 Iowa State University Alumni Survey
    A. Andrade, J. Eyles, Aakansha Jain, K. Kimle, Peter F. Orazem, and 1 more author
    2025
    Abs HTML

    Documents the design, sample construction, and descriptive findings of the 2024 Iowa State University alumni survey, a follow-up to the 2008 survey (Jolly, Yu, and Orazem 2009). Covers cohorts graduating from 1982 to 2023, with over 5,000 usable Bachelor’s-degree responses. Reports patterns of entrepreneurship, employment, education, and geographic mobility across 41 years of ISU graduates.

Works in Progress

  1. Does Broadband Close the Rural Education Gap?
    Aakansha Jain, Luyi Han, and John V. Winters
    2026
    Abs

    Asks whether federally subsidized broadband raises college enrollment among rural high-school graduates. The Connect America Fund Phase II set block eligibility ex ante through an FCC cost model and deployed in stages over 2015-2021, leaving nearly half of eligible blocks unserved as controls. A staggered difference-in-differences design with block and cohort fixed effects links deployment records to restricted American Community Survey microdata, accessed through the Iowa State Federal Statistical Research Data Center (Project 3044).

  2. Returns to Farming Among Iowa State Alumni
    Insoo Cho, Aakansha Jain, and Peter F. Orazem
    2026
    Abs

    Compares the household income of farmers and observationally similar nonfarm workers among Iowa State University graduates, using matching estimators and an endogenous treatment effects model. Farmers earn significantly less, even though household income includes off-farm earnings. Among all farmers the gap is concentrated in nonrural households; among full-time farmers it holds in rural areas as well. Entry into farming is driven mainly by family farming background.

  3. Firm Entry and Serial Entrepreneurship Among Iowa State Alumni
    Aakansha Jain, Daniel Lin, and Peter F. Orazem
    2026
    Abs

    Examines how labor market conditions at graduation shape entrepreneurial entry among Iowa State University alumni across the 2008 and 2024 alumni surveys. About 20 percent of graduates start a for-profit business, and one in four of those start more than one. A higher Iowa unemployment rate at graduation delays business entry for about a decade, after which the effect fades; national conditions show no such effect. Over a full career, family and social ties to business ownership predict who becomes an entrepreneur.
